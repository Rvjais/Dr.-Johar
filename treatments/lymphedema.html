Overview

About Lymphedema

Lymphedema is chronic swelling — usually of an arm or leg — caused by damage to or malformation of the lymphatic system, often after cancer surgery or radiotherapy. Untreated, it can lead to heaviness, infections and hardening of the tissue.

Treatment ranges from complete decongestive therapy and compression to modern microsurgical procedures — lymphaticovenous anastomosis (LVA), vascularised lymph node transfer and debulking liposuction — that can significantly reduce swelling and improve quality of life.

Benefits

  • Reduces limb volume and heaviness
  • Fewer episodes of cellulitis
  • Microsurgical options where conservative care fails
  • Coordinated with oncology and physiotherapy

Ideal candidates

  • Arm swelling after breast cancer treatment
  • Leg swelling after pelvic surgery or radiotherapy
  • Primary (congenital) lymphedema
  • Recurrent infections in a swollen limb
Your Journey

What to expect

  1. Assessment

    Clinical staging and lymphatic imaging.

  2. Conservative care

    Compression, manual drainage and skin care.

  3. Surgery

    LVA, lymph node transfer or liposuction depending on stage.

  4. Maintenance

    Ongoing compression and follow-up.

A note from Dr. Johar: Every patient is different. The details above are a general guide — your consultation will give you a personalised plan, timeline and estimate.
